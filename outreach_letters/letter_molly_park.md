Dear Commissioner Park,

I am writing to you as a member of the NYC Health + Hospitals Board of Directors because I have built something directly relevant to a decision your board just made — and it costs zero dollars.

NYC Health + Hospitals terminated its contract with Palantir. A defense contractor that was scanning patient health notes will no longer have access to the medical records of the most vulnerable New Yorkers. That was the right call.

But you know better than most people what happens next. You spent 20 years in affordable housing and social services. You ran DSS, which serves the same population that fills the beds at Bellevue, Kings County, Lincoln, and Elmhurst. The people in the NYC H+H system are the same people in the shelter system, the same people on SNAP and Medicaid, the same people your CityFHEPS program helped place in permanent housing. You once said the one thing that connects every person experiencing homelessness is that they do not have affordable housing. The one thing that connects every patient in a public hospital is that they cannot afford to go anywhere else.

These patients cannot afford for their hospital system to lose AI capabilities because the only options on the market send their data to corporate servers. And they certainly cannot afford another Palantir — another corporation with surveillance ties gaining access to their most intimate medical information.

I built a system that solves this completely.

It is an open-source distributed AI system where every machine runs its own independent AI model, entirely inside the hospital, on computers the hospital already owns. No patient data ever leaves the building. Not to any cloud. Not to any company. Not to any server outside the hospital walls. Zero external data transmission.

How it works: a local AI model acts as an automated coordinator. It takes a task — analyzing patient notes, drafting discharge summaries, reviewing records — breaks it into independent sub-tasks, and distributes each one to a separate computer on the internal network. Each computer processes its sub-task independently. When done, the coordinator combines the results. The entire pipeline runs behind the hospital's firewall.

This is not a product. This is not a startup. This is open-source, free, built by one person in one week, and it works. I am a software developer with years of experience, an independent inventor, and the author of 17 books on breakthroughs in science and technology.

You are a pragmatist. You think in terms of systems, budgets, and outcomes. So here are the numbers: Palantir cost nearly $4 million since 2023. My system costs zero. No licensing fees. No vendor contracts. No procurement process. It runs on hardware the hospitals already own. As someone who managed one of the nation's largest Section 8 programs and who increased housing placements by 24% in a single year, you know what it means when a solution is both better and free.
