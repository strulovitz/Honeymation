## THE SECRETARY PROBLEM

The secretary's mental model:
- "Is this a patient wanting an appointment?" → Handle it
- "Is this from the hospital administration/board?" → MUST forward to doctor
- "Is this from another doctor?" → MUST forward
- "Is this something I don't understand but sounds important/official/money-related?" → Better forward it, don't want to get in trouble
- "Is this a sales pitch / spam / cold email?" → Delete

**What gets DELETED:** Anything that looks like marketing, cold outreach, product pitch, "check out my startup"

**What gets FORWARDED:** Anything that sounds:
- Official/institutional (from the board, from administration, from NYC government)
- Money-related (budget, cost savings, bonus, grant)
- From another doctor or medical institution
- Compliance/legal (HIPAA, audit, regulation)
- Related to a KNOWN institutional decision (like the Palantir termination)
- Something she doesn't understand but sounds important — she forwards it because she's AFRAID of getting blamed if she deleted something the doctor needed

## SECRETARY-PROOF SUBJECT LINE STRATEGY

### Approach 1: Reference the KNOWN Palantir decision (institutional + money + she's heard of it)
The Palantir termination was announced at a city council meeting. It was in the news. The secretary may have heard about it in internal communications. Referencing it makes the email sound like it's part of an ongoing institutional process.

**Subject lines:**
- `Re: Palantir AI replacement — zero-cost alternative available for [Hospital Name] [Department]`
- `Following up on the Palantir contract termination — free AI system for [Hospital Name]`

⚠️ PROBLEM: Using "Re:" when there's no prior thread is deceptive. DON'T DO THIS.

### Approach 2: Sound like internal hospital administration (money + official)
Frame it as if it's about budget/cost savings that the doctor's department could benefit from.

**Subject lines:**
- `Cost elimination opportunity: AI system at $0 for [Department] — replaces $4M Palantir contract`
- `Budget notification: zero-cost AI alternative now available for [Hospital Name] departments`

### Approach 3: Make it incomprehensible but important-sounding (she forwards because she's afraid NOT to)
Use enough technical medical + AI jargon that the secretary thinks "I don't understand this but it sounds like something Dr. X needs to see."

**Subject lines:**
- `HIPAA-compliant on-premise distributed AI for clinical data analysis — zero external data transmission — for Dr. [Name]`
- `Air-gapped AI system for radiology/oncology — no patient data leaves hospital network — Dr. [Name]`

### Approach 4: The "another doctor/institution" angle
Make it look like it's from a fellow medical professional or research institution, not a tech person.

**Subject lines:**
- `From a fellow healthcare innovator to Dr. [Name]: on-premise AI that never touches patient data`
- `Physician-to-physician: new AI approach for [Department] — zero cloud dependency`

### ⭐ RECOMMENDED APPROACH: Combine #2 and #3

**THE WINNING SUBJECT LINE:**

```
For Dr. [Last Name]: $0 AI system replacing Palantir — HIPAA-compliant, no patient data leaves [Hospital Name]
```

**Why this works on the secretary:**
1. **"For Dr. [Last Name]"** — addressed to the doctor by name, she MUST forward it
2. **"$0"** — MONEY. Cost savings. The doctor AND the hospital care about this.
3. **"replacing Palantir"** — references a KNOWN institutional decision she may have heard about
4. **"HIPAA-compliant"** — compliance/legal word. She's been trained to not ignore HIPAA stuff.
5. **"no patient data leaves"** — sounds like a security/compliance matter
6. **She doesn't fully understand it** — but it sounds official, mentions money, mentions compliance, mentions a known company. She forwards it.

**Why this works on the DOCTOR:**
1. **$0** — free. No budget needed. No procurement process.
2. **Replacing Palantir** — they KNOW Palantir is gone. They KNOW they need a replacement.
3. **HIPAA-compliant** — their #1 concern with ANY AI system
4. **No data leaves** — solves the exact problem that killed Palantir
5. **Implicit opportunity** — the doctor who brings this to the CEO looks like a hero, gets recognition, potential bonus/promotion
