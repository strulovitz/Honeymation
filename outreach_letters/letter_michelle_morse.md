Dear Dr. Morse,

I am writing to you because you are one of the very few people in American healthcare who would immediately understand why this matters — and what to do about it.

NYC Health + Hospitals just terminated its contract with Palantir. A defense contractor with ties to ICE and military surveillance was scanning patient health notes across the largest public hospital system in America. The right decision was made to end that. But now there is a gap — and the patients who depend on this system cannot afford to wait.

You have spent your career fighting for the people who get left behind. You dismantled a racially biased kidney algorithm that was hurting Black patients across the country. You co-founded EqualHealth to build healthcare capacity in Haiti. You created the Campaign Against Racism with 23 chapters in 10 countries. You declared racism a public health crisis as acting commissioner. And now, back as Chief Medical Officer, you are building data-driven tools to compare how hospitals serve Medicaid and uninsured patients.

Here is the problem with every AI system that NYC H+H could adopt to replace Palantir: they all send patient data outside the hospital. To a cloud. To a company. To servers controlled by corporations with their own interests. For the million New Yorkers who depend on public hospitals — many of them undocumented, uninsured, Black, Latino, the most vulnerable people in the city — that is not a theoretical risk. It is a real one. Their medical records, their mental health notes, their HIV status, their immigration-related injuries — all flowing through corporate servers.

I built a system that makes this problem disappear.

It is an open-source distributed AI system where every machine runs its own independent AI model, entirely inside the hospital, on computers the hospital already owns. No patient data ever leaves the building. Not to any cloud. Not to any company. Not to any server outside the hospital walls. There is zero external data transmission. Zero.

How it works: a local AI model acts as an automated coordinator. It takes a task — analyzing patient notes, drafting discharge summaries, reviewing radiology reports — breaks it into independent sub-tasks, and distributes each one to a separate computer on the hospital's internal network. Each computer processes its sub-task independently. When done, the coordinator combines the results. The entire pipeline runs behind the hospital's firewall.

This is not a product. This is not a startup. This is open-source, free, built by one person in one week, and it works. I am a software developer with years of experience, an independent inventor, and the author of 17 books on breakthroughs in science and technology.

Think about what this means for the patients you fight for every day. The undocumented mother who is afraid to seek prenatal care because she does not know where her data goes. The teenager in a mental health crisis whose records should never leave Kings County Hospital. The Medicaid patient whose entire medical history should not be sitting on a corporate server next to a government contract. With this system, their data stays inside the hospital. Period.

And it costs nothing. No licensing fees. No vendor contracts. No procurement process. No corporate dependency. The opposite of Palantir in every way.
