Right now, when your viewers use ChatGPT, Google Gemini, or any cloud AI to research medical questions, plan their finances, write documents, or even look up survival information — every single query flows through corporate servers. These are servers owned by companies with government contracts, subject to data requests, and storing everything permanently. Their questions about medications, their financial plans, their political views, their location-specific emergency research — all sitting on someone else's computer.

And here is the part that matters for preparedness: if the internet goes down, their AI is gone. If a company changes its terms of service, their AI is gone. If a government decides to restrict access during a crisis — exactly when people need information most — their AI is gone. Cloud AI is a single point of failure, and your audience knows better than anyone that single points of failure will fail.

I built the solution.

I am a software developer and independent inventor. I built an open-source distributed AI system that runs entirely on your own computers, inside your own home, on your own local network. No cloud. No internet required. No corporate servers. No data ever leaving your house.

How it works: you install local AI models on computers you already own — a desktop, a laptop, even older machines. A stronger local AI model acts as a coordinator. You give it any task — research, writing, analysis, planning — and it breaks the task into independent pieces, sends each piece to a different computer on your home network, each machine processes its piece independently with zero communication between them, and the coordinator combines the results. Everything stays inside your house. Nothing leaves.

If you have solar power — which you have taught thousands of people to set up — your AI works when the grid is down. If the internet goes out, your AI still runs because it never needed the internet in the first place. It is the only AI system that is truly grid-independent.
