## Reply 3: Louis Rossmann (2M subs) — REJECTED

**Date:** April 6, 2026
**Who replied:** Louis Rossmann personally

**Their reply:**
> this was drafted by chatgpt..... no.

**Our letter:** Connected his Clippy protest movement to our system, positioned as "a tool worthy of the movement he started."

**What actually went wrong:** The letter made him feel SMALL. Despite multiple rewrites to soften it, the underlying structure still implied "your protest was symbolic, here's the real thing." His ego got bruised. The "chatgpt" comment was his way of dismissing it without admitting that.

**What did NOT go wrong:**
- AI writing is NOT the problem — everyone writes with AI today, it's standard
- Letter length is NOT the problem — interested people read, uninterested people don't
- The problem was violating the Golden Rule: we REPLACED his work instead of EXTENDING it

**Lesson:** The Golden Rule (Extend, Never Replace) is everything. Rossmann's rejection is proof. Do NOT shorten letters or try to "sound more human" — that's not the fix. The fix is making the recipient feel ELEVATED.

**What happened:** He's a busy Dean. He either didn't read the full letter or wasn't interested enough to engage with the content. Responded only to the "paid collaboration" ask at the bottom.

- It does NOT mean we should remove "paid collaboration" from institutional letters — Wooldridge had the same line and responded positively

- If someone IS interested, they read the letter. If not, no format change will force them to care.

**Lesson:** Some people won't be interested. That's OK. Don't handicap letters for the 90% who don't care — keep them strong for the 1-10% who DO care.

## Summary of What Works

| What works | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Mention their specific content (1-2 lines max) | NetworkChuck replied |
| Speak their exact professional language | Wooldridge replied |
| Connect to THEIR specific project/research | Wooldridge replied |
| Position as "next level" / extension of their work | NetworkChuck replied |
| Honest ask ("great video topic" / "value your evaluation") | Both positive replies |
| Long, detailed letters (20-30 sentences) | Both positive replies were to long letters |
| "Paid collaboration" line for institutions | Wooldridge had it and still replied positively |

| What doesn't work | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Making recipient feel small / replacing their work | Rossmann rejected |
| Deep dossier (10+ facts about them) | Multiple non-responses |
| Generic institutional emails (not named individuals) | ~81 contacts to Asia, zero replies |

## Rules for ALL future letters

1. **Golden Rule: Extend, Never Replace** — their work is the foundation, ours adds a layer
2. **Invention is the hook** — lead with what we built, not politics or their biography
3. **"Your data never leaves the building"** — this is the key privacy message (NOT "no single model sees the whole task" which is factually wrong — the Queen Bee sees the whole task)
4. **Keep letters long** — the idea needs 20-30 sentences to land. Short letters kill the message for the people who WOULD be interested
5. **Keep using AI to write** — it's standard, not a problem. The content matters, not whether AI helped write it
6. **For YouTubers:** suggest it as a video topic for their audience
7. **For institutions:** keep "paid collaboration" line — we are not volunteers
8. **One or two personal touches max** — not a dossier
9. **Be vulnerable, ask don't tell** — "would you take a look?" not "your country needs this"
10. **Position them as the judge** — "I genuinely don't know if this is as important as I think"
